## Office Hours Output

**Core insight:**  
"College interviewers and friends saying 'that's a cool idea' is polite applause, not customer demand—without a desperate buyer willing to pay or put in the work to write hundreds of glyphs, building an Indic font engine is an expensive technical hobby masquerading as a startup."

**Signal strength:**  
**WEAK**  
The founder could not name a single paying customer, could not identify a narrow paid wedge, and has collected zero pre-commitments or real-world user research. The current motivation rests entirely on polite encouragement from friends and university lab interviewers.

**Refined scope:**  
"As a **Bengali/Hindi visual designer or wedding stationer**, I need **a 1-click way to turn a 50-letter sample of my custom lettering into a reusable display font** so that **I can brand client materials without manually drawing every headline in Illustrator.**"

**Biggest open risk:**  
The **onboarding barrier vs. utility tradeoff**: In English, free mature tools (like FontCrafter) exist; in Indic scripts, users will abandon the process immediately if asked to write 300–1,000 conjuncts/ligatures by hand unless an automated extrapolation engine or narrow headline-only scope solves it for them.

**Recommended next step:**  
**Get 3 pre-commitments manually before writing code.**  
Take 3 designers or creative friends. Have them send you a photo of their handwriting on paper. Manually trace and build a 40-character TTF file for them by hand using existing tools (Calligraphr/Glyphs). Charge them ₹500 / $10 each or ask them to use it in a real project and post about it. If they won't use it or pay when you do the manual grunt work for them, software won't fix it.

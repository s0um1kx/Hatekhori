The Indic Typography Problem: Solved

Over 600 million people speak Bengali and Hindi, yet every Western handwriting font tool treats Indic languages as an afterthought. Indic scripts cannot be set with simple Latin baselines—they require accurate glyph closure:

  • Vowel Signs (Matras / কার / मात्रा): Placed above, below, before, or split around consonants.
  • Conjuncts (Yuktakshars / যুক্তবর্ণ / संयुक्ताक्षर): Fused ligatures that change form entirely when letters combine.
  • Continuous Topline (Shirorekha): Preserving handwritten fluid connections between Devanagari letters.

Hatekhori's computational typography pipeline encodes these OpenType GSUB and GPOS lookup tables directly into the compiled font.
